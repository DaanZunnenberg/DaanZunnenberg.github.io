    <h3>The Tukey depth process under short range dependence</h3>
    <span class="entry-date">2026 &middot; draft</span>
  </div>
  <div class="entry-org">
    <span class="pub-authors">Zunnenberg, D. &amp; D&uuml;rre, A.</span>
    <span class="pub-venue">Unpublished manuscript</span>
  </div>
  <div class="readme-toggle">
    <button type="button" class="readme-summary" aria-expanded="false">
      <span class="label-open">+ Show abstract</span><span class="label-close">&minus; Hide abstract</span>
    </button>
    <div class="readme-collapse">
      <div class="readme">
        <p>
          We extend the theory of Tukey depth to the setting of absolutely regular (\(\beta\)-mixing) stochastic
          processes. In particular, we generalize Mass&eacute;'s weak convergence result for the Tukey depth
          process to dependent observations and establish convergence rates expressed in terms of the mixing
          coefficients. An application extends the theory developed by Mass&eacute; on depth weighted estimators.
          First, we show that the assumptions on the weight can be significantly weakened, and secondly we show
          that the observations can be taken as weakly dependent. A simulation study demonstrates the advantages
          of depth weighted estimators under heavy tailed innovations.
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>

    <h3>Absolute regularity and maximal moment inequalities</h3>
    <span class="entry-date">2026 &middot; in preparation</span>
  </div>
  <div class="entry-org">
    <span class="pub-authors">Zunnenberg, D. &amp; D&uuml;rre, A.</span>
    <span class="pub-venue">Unpublished manuscript</span>
  </div>
  <div class="readme-toggle">
    <button type="button" class="readme-summary" aria-expanded="false">
      <span class="label-open">+ Show abstract</span><span class="label-close">&minus; Hide abstract</span>
    </button>
    <div class="readme-collapse">
      <div class="readme">
        <p>
          This article establishes maximal moment inequalities for random variables satisfying absolute
          regularity, uniformly over possibly infinite \(\mathcal{F}\). Our approach combines admissible
          partition sequences theory with Pollard's truncation technique and recursive Berbee&ndash;Goldstein
          maximal coupling. We discuss regularity via the Ossiander entropy integral and explain the structural
          connection with the Boucheron&ndash;Bousquet&ndash;Lugosi&ndash;Massart inequality. Concentration
          properties are similar to those of Johnson&ndash;Schechtman&ndash;Zinn and Kwapie&#324;&ndash;Szulga up
          to possibly enlarged norms.
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

  <div class="entry-head">
    <h3>Absolute regularity and maximal moment inequalities II: Koltchinskii&ndash;Pollard entropy</h3>
    <span class="entry-date">2026 &middot; in preparation</span>
  </div>
  <div class="entry-org">
    <span class="pub-authors">Zunnenberg, D. &amp; D&uuml;rre, A.</span>
    <span class="pub-venue">Unpublished manuscript</span>
  </div>
  <div class="readme-toggle">
    <button type="button" class="readme-summary" aria-expanded="false">
      <span class="label-open">+ Show abstract</span><span class="label-close">&minus; Hide abstract</span>
    </button>
    <div class="readme-collapse">
      <div class="readme">
        <p>
          This article gives a uniform moment inequality for empirical processes indexed by measurable functions
          arising from absolutely regular random variables. The bound involves, as is the case in the classical
          Koltchinskii&ndash;Pollard inequality, the uniform entropy integral with an additional factor
          controlling the minimal measurable cover function. The norm in which this envelope is expressed is
          related to the dependent Marcinkiewicz&ndash;Zygmund inequalities. A detailed overview of minimal
          requirements to ensure measurability and finiteness of the respective Rosenthal type norms is provided.
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

  <div class="entry-head">
    <h3>Distributed Coherent Radio Frequency Spectrum Operations</h3>
    <span class="entry-date">Proceedings</span>
  </div>
  <div class="entry-org">
    <span class="pub-authors">Choudhary, H., Dombrowsky, C., Eijsvoogel, B., Hupkes, H.J., van der Knaap, V., Luesink, E., Metherall, B., Muskulus, M., Rice, S., Stagnaro, P. &amp; Zunnenberg, D.</span>
    <span class="pub-venue">Proceedings of the Study Group Mathematics with Industry</span>
  </div>
  <div class="readme-toggle">
    <button type="button" class="readme-summary" aria-expanded="false">
      <span class="label-open">+ Show abstract</span><span class="label-close">&minus; Hide abstract</span>
    </button>
    <div class="readme-collapse">
      <div class="readme">
        <p>
          Software defined radios spread over a region can act as a single antenna array, provided they are
          synchronized to within a fraction of a sample. This requires accurate estimates of the propagation
          delays between the radios, which have to be obtained from a received signal that is a noisy
          superposition of a direct path and several reflections. We study this delay estimation problem for the
          training field (L-LTF) of a WiFi preamble. We first review the MUSIC, ESPRIT and matrix pencil methods
          and derive a Cram&eacute;r&ndash;Rao lower bound for the delay estimates. We then show how the singular
          value decompositions in these methods can be replaced by a randomized singular value decomposition, and
          test this numerically. Finally, we propose two alternatives that avoid matrix decompositions altogether.
          NewDiR maximizes a function of the covariance matrix directly, performing a few Newton iterations
          initiated from cross-correlation peaks. Template matching works entirely in the time domain and extends
          naturally to time-varying channels.
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

    <h3><a href="https://www.econstor.eu/handle/10419/316193" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Functional location-scale models with robust observation-driven dynamics</a></h3>
    <span class="entry-date">2025</span>
  </div>
  <div class="entry-org">
    <span class="pub-authors">Lin, Y. &amp; Lucas, A.</span>
    <span class="pub-venue">Tinbergen Institute Discussion Paper No. TI 2025-027/III</span>
  </div>
  <div class="readme-toggle">
    <button type="button" class="readme-summary" aria-expanded="false">
      <span class="label-open">+ Show abstract</span><span class="label-close">&minus; Hide abstract</span>
    </button>
    <div class="readme-collapse">
      <div class="readme">
        <p>
          We introduce a new class of location-scale models for dynamic functional data in arbitrary but fixed
          dimensions, where the location and scale functional parameters can evolve over time. A key feature of
          the parameter dynamics in these models is its observation-driven nature, where the one-step-ahead
          evolution is fully determined conditional on past observations, yet remains stochastic unconditionally.
          We estimate the model using a likelihood-based approach designed for sparsely observed data and
          establish the consistency and asymptotic normality of the underlying static parameters that govern the
          location-scale dynamics. The choice of objective function and the construction of the dynamics together
          shield the time-varying location and scale parameters from the potentially distorting effects of
          influential observations. Simulations reveal that our method can recover the unobserved location-scale
          dynamics from sparse data, even in the presence of model mis-specification and substantial outliers. We
          apply our framework to examine the intraday volatility dynamics of Pfizer stock returns during the
          COVID-19 pandemic, and PM2.5 concentrations measured by low-cost sensors across Europe. The proposed
          model exhibits robust performance in capturing dynamics for both datasets despite the presence of many
          large shocks.
        </p>
      </div>
    </div>
